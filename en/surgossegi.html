What counts as an emergency?

If your pet shows any of these signs — don't wait.

In many cases, every minute counts. If you're unsure, call — we'll help you decide over the phone whether treatment is needed.

Difficulty breathing
Panting at rest, blue tongue or gums, signs of choking.
Severe bleeding
Uncontrollable bleeding from anywhere, lasting more than 5 minutes.
Loss of consciousness or seizure
Any prolonged seizure (over 1 minute), or fainting.
Suspected poisoning
Chocolate, medication, plants, chemicals, antifreeze — anything not meant to be eaten.
Trauma or accident
Car accident, fall, bite — even if there's no visible injury.
Vomiting or diarrhea with blood
Repeated symptoms containing blood within 24 hours.
Bloat
Sudden, hard, swollen belly, unproductive attempts to vomit.
Heatstroke
Heavy panting, overheating, weakness in summer heat.
Before You Arrive

What should you do before you leave?

Four simple steps that can help a lot — for you and your pet.

Emergency line
01
Call us first
Don't head out without calling. A phone consultation lets the vet prepare before you even arrive.
02
Stay calm
Your pet senses your stress. Speak to them calmly and avoid sudden movements.
03
Keep them warm and calm
Cover them with a towel or blanket. In case of injury, minimize movement.
04
Bring everything with you
Vaccination booklet, medication list, previous records — everything helps with a fast diagnosis.

Outside Office Hours

After 7 PM on weekdays, and on weekends and holidays, call the emergency line. The vet on duty will call you back.
